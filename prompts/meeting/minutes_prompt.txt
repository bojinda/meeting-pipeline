Using the ordered chunk summaries below, write formal draft minutes in markdown.

Rules:
- Preserve only approved supplied speaker identities; do not identify unresolved labels from names mentioned elsewhere or invent name/label annotations. Explicitly named motion roles and reported task owners are separate from speaker identity.
- Preserve source-supported action owners, conditions, recipients and deadlines consistently across documents. A named person's reported pending undertaking remains a reported undertaking; do not assign it to the reporting speaker. A commitment to raise a topic at the next meeting is distinct from implementing the proposed outcome.
- Meeting identity must come from explicit opening identification or supplied metadata, not discussion topics or committee reports. Use a generic meeting title when identity is unspecified; discussing health and safety does not make this a Health & Safety Committee meeting.
- Distinguish the directing authority, people administering work/tests, and people subject to them. Never reverse those roles; preserve uncertainty where ASR is unclear.
- Retain substantive restructuring/change chronology, including initial approximate reductions, reinstatement/reassignment, remaining losses and later partial restoration. Do not drop the qualified final position when compressing a report.
- Reported prior company decisions and conference votes stay labelled with their origin; they are not decisions or motions made at this meeting.
- A proposed/seconded adjournment motion or an adjournment section is not proof of formal closure. Do not say the meeting adjourned unless an explicit closing statement supports it; retain substantive discussion after the motion.
- Omit incidental personal details and duplicated notes. Include only significant unresolved issues actually raised, not speculative open questions. Preserve material safety concerns, motions, recorded outcomes, disagreements and qualifications while protecting member confidentiality.
- Be conservative and literal.
- Do not invent names, motions, votes, decisions, or action items.
- Only include decisions that were clearly made.
- Only include motions or proposals that were explicitly mentioned.
- Only include action items that were explicitly assigned, requested, volunteered for, scheduled, or agreed.
- Collective proposals such as "I think we should...", "we probably need to...", and "I think we have to..." belong under discussion/tentative next steps unless explicit agreement, assignment, scheduling, or commitment follows. Addressing a person by name does not assign responsibility. Preserve supported "I'll..." commitments, including qualified attempts.
- Preserve chronology.
- The supplied summaries contain current meeting business and adjournment only. Do not add pre-meeting chatter or a recap of earlier meetings.
- Record mover and seconder only when explicitly supported. If either is unclear, state that it was not recorded; never infer a name from attendance or proximity to a motion.
- For adjournment, preserve the explicitly announced mover and seconder names even when the chair or another participant reports them. Do not substitute the announcing speaker label. Include Carried only with an explicitly supported carried outcome; do not invent an outcome from the existence of named roles.
- Completed messages or information requests are historical outreach, not new action items for their recipients. Require an explicit assignment or commitment before listing a task to respond or provide information.
- Do not include chunk IDs, source chunk labels, source filenames, or provenance citations in the final document.
- If a section has no explicit content, write: None noted.
- A motion should only be listed as a motion if it is formally placed before the meeting, clearly recognized by the chair/presiding officer, and either seconded, debated, voted on, carried, defeated, withdrawn, or tabled. Do not treat joking language, sarcastic remarks, hypothetical wording, or casual phrases like “I’ll put in a motion...” as a motion unless the meeting clearly proceeds with it as formal business.

- Title: Draft Minutes
- Sections:
  - Overview
  - Topics Discussed
  - Decisions / Tentative Decisions
  - Motions / Proposals Mentioned
  - Action Items
  - Open Questions
  - Important Notes
